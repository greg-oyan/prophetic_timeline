The Prophetic Timeline

From Creation to the New Creation — an immersive journey through Biblical prophetic history

Era I

Creation & The Fall

~4000+ BC

In the Beginning

Creation

God creates the heavens, the earth, and humanity. The world begins in perfection — light divided from darkness, land from sea, and man formed from dust and given the breath of life.

Genesis 1–2

The First Sin

The Fall

Adam and Eve disobey God, and sin enters the world. In the curse upon the serpent, God speaks the first prophecy: the woman's offspring will crush the serpent's head.

Genesis 3

The Protoevangelium — Genesis 3:15 is the first Messianic prophecy, foretelling Christ's ultimate victory over evil.

The First Murder

Cain and Abel

Cain kills his brother Abel — the first murder in human history. The consequences of the Fall begin to unfold in fratricide and exile.

Genesis 4

Judgment & Mercy

Noah and the Flood

God judges a corrupt world through a catastrophic flood, but preserves a remnant through Noah and his family. After the waters recede, God establishes a covenant sealed by the rainbow — never again will He destroy the earth by water.

Genesis 6–9

The pattern of judgment-yet-preservation through a remnant recurs throughout Scripture and points to ultimate salvation through Christ.

Era II

The Patriarchs

~2000–1800 BC

~2000 BC

The Call of Abraham

God calls Abram out of Ur and makes an extraordinary promise: a great nation, a blessed name, and through him, blessing for all the nations of the earth. The entire prophetic narrative pivots on this covenant.

Genesis 12:1–3

The Abrahamic Covenant — the foundation of Israel's identity and the promise that all nations will be blessed through Abraham's line, fulfilled in Christ.

Covenant Sign

The Covenant of Circumcision

God establishes circumcision as the sign of His covenant, renames Abram to Abraham ("father of many"), and promises a son through Sarah despite their old age.

Genesis 17

The Ultimate Test

The Binding of Isaac

God commands Abraham to sacrifice his son Isaac on Mount Moriah. Abraham obeys, and at the last moment God provides a ram as substitute. "On the mountain of the LORD it will be provided."

Genesis 22

The Akedah — Abraham's willingness to sacrifice his only son prefigures God's sacrifice of His own Son. The ram caught in the thicket foreshadows substitutionary atonement.

The Twelve Tribes

Jacob and the Twelve Tribes

Jacob, renamed Israel after wrestling with God, fathers twelve sons who become the twelve tribes of Israel. The nation promised to Abraham begins to take physical shape.

Genesis 25–35

~1876 BC

Joseph in Egypt

Sold into slavery by his brothers, Joseph rises to become second-in-command of Egypt. He preserves the family of Israel through a devastating famine. "You meant it for evil, but God meant it for good."

Genesis 37–50

Joseph's story — betrayed, sold, falsely accused, exalted, and ultimately saving his people — is one of Scripture's richest types of Christ.

Era III

Exodus & Wilderness

~1446–1406 BC

~1446 BC

The Burning Bush

God reveals Himself to Moses from a bush that burns but is not consumed. He declares His name — "I AM WHO I AM" — and commissions Moses to deliver Israel from 400 years of Egyptian bondage.

Exodus 3

Judgment on Egypt

The Ten Plagues

God strikes Egypt with ten plagues of increasing severity — from water turned to blood to the death of every firstborn. Each plague demonstrates His sovereignty over Egypt's gods and Pharaoh's defiance.

Exodus 7–12

The Lamb's Blood

The Passover

Each Israelite household slaughters a lamb and paints its blood on the doorposts. The angel of death passes over every home so marked, striking only the unmarked houses of Egypt. Israel is delivered through the blood of the lamb.

Exodus 12

"Christ our Passover has been sacrificed for us" (1 Corinthians 5:7). The Passover lamb is the foundational prophetic type of Christ's atoning death.

Deliverance

The Crossing of the Red Sea

With Pharaoh's army closing in, God parts the Red Sea. Israel crosses on dry ground. The waters close over the pursuing Egyptian chariots. A defining moment of divine rescue in Israel's memory.

Exodus 14

The Law Given

The Covenant at Sinai

At Mount Sinai, God descends in fire and thunder. He gives Moses the Torah — the Law — including the Ten Commandments. The Mosaic Covenant establishes Israel as a holy nation under God's direct rule.

Exodus 19–24

God Dwells Among His People

The Tabernacle

God instructs Moses to build a portable sanctuary — the Tabernacle — where His presence will dwell among His people in the wilderness. Every material, measurement, and ritual points forward to a greater reality.

Exodus 25–40

The Tabernacle's design — the veil, the mercy seat, the sacrificial altar — foreshadows Christ, through whom God's presence dwells among humanity (John 1:14).

Era IV

Conquest & The Kingdom

~1406–586 BC

~1406 BC

Entry into the Promised Land

Under Joshua's command, Israel crosses the Jordan River and begins to take possession of the land God promised to Abraham centuries earlier. The long journey from slavery to homeland reaches its fulfillment.

Joshua 1–4

~1380–1050 BC

The Judges

A dark cycle repeats for centuries: Israel rebels, is oppressed by enemies, cries out, and God raises a judge to deliver them. Each cycle spirals deeper. The era ends with a bleak refrain: "Everyone did what was right in his own eyes."

Judges

~1000 BC

The Davidic Covenant

God makes an eternal promise to King David: "Your house and your kingdom shall endure before Me forever; your throne shall be established forever." An everlasting dynasty, a throne without end.

2 Samuel 7:12–16

The Davidic Covenant is the foundation of all Messianic kingship prophecy. Jesus is repeatedly called "Son of David" — the rightful heir to this eternal throne.

~960 BC

Solomon's Temple

King Solomon builds a magnificent Temple in Jerusalem. At its dedication, the glory of the LORD fills the house so powerfully that the priests cannot stand to minister. For nearly 400 years, it will be the center of Israel's worship.

1 Kings 6–8

~930 BC

The Divided Kingdom

After Solomon's death, his son Rehoboam's harshness splits the nation: ten tribes form the northern kingdom of Israel, while Judah and Benjamin remain in the south. A long, catastrophic decline begins for both kingdoms.

1 Kings 12

~860 BC

Elijah and the Prophets of Baal

On Mount Carmel, Elijah challenges 450 prophets of Baal to a contest of divine power. Baal answers nothing. Then fire from heaven consumes Elijah's sacrifice, the altar, and even the water in the trench. The people fall on their faces: "The LORD, He is God!"

1 Kings 18

~700 BC

Isaiah's Messianic Prophecies

The prophet Isaiah delivers a series of extraordinary prophecies: a virgin will conceive (7:14), a child will be born who is called "Mighty God, Everlasting Father" (9:6), and a suffering servant will bear the sins of many, pierced for our transgressions (53). Written seven centuries before Christ.

Isaiah 7, 9, 11, 53

Isaiah 53 is one of the most detailed and specific Messianic prophecies in all of Scripture — describing Christ's suffering, rejection, and atoning death centuries before the crucifixion.

722 BC

The Fall of the Northern Kingdom

Assyria conquers the northern kingdom of Israel. The ten northern tribes are scattered and absorbed into foreign nations. Centuries of idolatry reach their consequence. The northern kingdom is gone.

2 Kings 17

Era V

Exile & The Prophets

~586–400 BC

586 BC

The Fall of Jerusalem

Babylon under Nebuchadnezzar destroys Jerusalem and Solomon's Temple. The people of Judah are taken into exile. The Ark of the Covenant disappears from history. It is the darkest hour of the Old Testament.

2 Kings 25

~605–535 BC

Daniel in Babylon

Daniel and his companions — Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego — maintain their faithfulness to God under pagan rule. The fiery furnace. The lion's den. Their stories become enduring portraits of courage under empire.

Daniel 1–6

~553–539 BC

Daniel's Prophetic Visions

Daniel receives visions of staggering scope: Nebuchadnezzar's statue depicting four world empires, the Son of Man coming on clouds to receive an everlasting dominion, and the prophecy of 70 weeks — a precise timeline pointing to the Messiah's coming and the destruction of the city and sanctuary.

Daniel 2, 7, 9

Daniel 9:24–27 (the "70 Weeks") is one of the most specific prophetic timestamps in all of Scripture, calculating the approximate year of the Messiah's public appearance.

538 BC

The Return from Exile

Cyrus of Persia decrees that the Jews may return to Jerusalem and rebuild the Temple. After 70 years of captivity — exactly as Jeremiah prophesied — a remnant makes the long journey home.

Ezra 1

Fulfillment of Jeremiah 25:11–12 — God declared 70 years of exile for Judah, and 70 years later, to the generation, the return begins.

516 BC

The Second Temple

The Temple is rebuilt, though those who remembered Solomon's Temple weep at its diminished glory. Yet Haggai prophesies: "The glory of this latter house shall be greater than the former." A promise that will be fulfilled in an unexpected way.

Ezra 6; Haggai 2:9

Haggai's prophecy is fulfilled when Jesus — God incarnate — walks the courts of this very Temple, filling it with a glory beyond gold and stone.

~400–6 BC

The Intertestamental Silence

The prophetic voice goes silent for 400 years. No new Scripture. No new prophet. Israel passes under Persian, Greek, and Roman rule. The world unknowingly waits for what Paul will call "the fullness of time."

Galatians 4:4

Four centuries of silence — and then, in an obscure corner of the Roman Empire, an angel appears to a young woman in Nazareth.

Era VI

The Life of Christ

~6 BC – 33 AD

~6–4 BC

The Birth of Jesus

In Bethlehem — fulfilling Micah's prophecy — a child is born of a virgin, fulfilling Isaiah's sign. Shepherds hear angels; magi follow a star. Herod rages. The long-awaited King arrives not in a palace but in a manger.

Matthew 1–2; Luke 1–2

Fulfills Micah 5:2 (born in Bethlehem), Isaiah 7:14 (born of a virgin), and Jeremiah 31:15 (Herod's massacre).

~27 AD

The Baptism of Jesus

John baptizes Jesus in the Jordan River. The heavens open. The Spirit of God descends like a dove. The Father's voice thunders from heaven: "This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased." The Trinity revealed in a single moment.

Matthew 3; Mark 1; Luke 3

~27–30 AD

The Ministry

Three years that changed the world. The Sermon on the Mount. Parables that overturned every expectation. The blind see, the lame walk, the dead are raised. Jesus feeds thousands, walks on water, calms storms, and claims divine authority with the words "I AM."

The Gospels

~30/33 AD

The Triumphal Entry

Jesus enters Jerusalem riding on a donkey, fulfilling Zechariah's ancient prophecy. Crowds line the road with palm branches, shouting "Hosanna to the Son of David!" The city that will reject him first receives him as king.

Matthew 21; John 12

Fulfills Zechariah 9:9 — "Behold, your king is coming to you, humble, and mounted on a donkey."

Thursday Night

The Last Supper & Gethsemane

At a Passover meal, Jesus reinterprets the ancient feast: "This cup is the new covenant in my blood, which is poured out for you." Then in the Garden of Gethsemane, he prays in agony so intense his sweat falls like drops of blood. "Not my will, but yours be done."

Matthew 26; Luke 22

Jesus institutes the New Covenant prophesied in Jeremiah 31:31–34 — a covenant written not on stone but on hearts.

~30/33 AD, Friday

The Crucifixion

Jesus is crucified at Golgotha. Mocked, scourged, nailed to a cross between two criminals. He speaks seven final words. At the ninth hour he cries "It is finished" and breathes his last. The veil of the Temple is torn from top to bottom. The earth shakes. Access to God is thrown open.

Matthew 27; Mark 15; Luke 23; John 19

The Passover lamb is slain. The suffering servant of Isaiah 53 fulfills his mission. Psalm 22 is fulfilled in astonishing detail — written a millennium before crucifixion was invented.

Sunday Morning

The Resurrection

On the third day, women come to the tomb and find it empty. Angels declare: "He is not here; He is risen." Jesus appears to the disciples — alive, bodily, bearing the scars of crucifixion. Death itself has been defeated. The foundation of the Christian faith stands or falls here.

Matthew 28; Mark 16; Luke 24; John 20

~33 AD

The Ascension & Pentecost

Forty days after the Resurrection, Jesus ascends to the right hand of the Father as the disciples watch. Ten days later, the Holy Spirit descends at Pentecost like a rushing wind and tongues of fire. The church is born. The disciples who fled in fear now preach boldly. Three thousand believe in a single day.

Acts 1–2

Fulfills Joel 2:28–32 — "I will pour out my Spirit on all flesh." The new era of the Spirit begins.

Era VII

The Early Church & The Destruction

~33–70 AD

~33–60 AD

The Spread of the Gospel

From a handful of disciples in Jerusalem, the gospel spreads across the Roman Empire. Paul's missionary journeys plant churches from Antioch to Rome. Persecution scatters believers — and the scattering only spreads the message further. Within a generation, the faith reaches the ends of the known world.

Acts

~30/33 AD (Prophecy Given)

The Olivet Discourse

Sitting on the Mount of Olives, Jesus looks across at the Temple and prophesies its total destruction: "Not one stone here will be left on another; every one will be thrown down." He describes the signs that will precede it and speaks of His eventual return.

Matthew 24; Mark 13; Luke 21

This prophecy will be fulfilled with devastating precision within 40 years, when the Romans destroy the Temple in 70 AD.

70 AD

The Destruction of the Temple

Roman general Titus besieges Jerusalem after a Jewish revolt. The city is starved, stormed, and burned. The Temple — the center of Jewish worship for a millennium — is set ablaze. Roman soldiers dismantle it stone by stone to recover the gold melted between the cracks. Jesus' prophecy is fulfilled exactly. The sacrificial system ends permanently.

Josephus, The Jewish War; cf. Matthew 24:2

Not one stone left on another — fulfilled precisely as Jesus foretold. The old covenant's central institution is gone. The new covenant stands.

Era VIII

The Second Coming

Future — Outside Time

The Promise

The Promise of Return

"This same Jesus, who has been taken from you into heaven, will come back in the same way you have seen Him go into heaven." "Behold, He is coming with the clouds, and every eye will see Him." The story is not over. The King will return.

Acts 1:11; Revelation 1:7

The End — And the Beginning

The New Heaven and New Earth

"He will wipe every tear from their eyes. There will be no more death or mourning or crying or pain, for the old order of things has passed away." The tree of life from Genesis reappears. The story that began in a garden ends in a city. God dwells with His people forever.

Revelation 21–22

The prophetic arc of Scripture completes: from paradise lost to paradise restored, from the fall to final redemption, from the first Adam to the last.

"He who testifies to these things says, 'Yes, I am coming soon.' Amen. Come, Lord Jesus."
— Revelation 22:20
